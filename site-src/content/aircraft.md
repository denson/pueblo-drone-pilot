# Aircraft options

## The first aircraft is modest, and chosen last

The revised plan starts with a relatively inexpensive, capable drone selected for the facility mission, not for county-wide range, and selected only after the site engineering answers the questions on the [first-site page](../first-site/). [[proposal]] Its priorities: a thermal camera if financially practical, useful optical zoom, good low-light performance, stable automated waypoint flight, reliable geofencing, return and land controls, wind tolerance appropriate to Pueblo West, Remote ID compliance, simple battery logistics, secure user accounts, and exportable flight logs and video. [[proposal]] No model has been short-listed, and this page deliberately names none until the site survey exists. [[open]]

## The long-endurance county layer, for the later phase

The aircraft below were the original concept's candidates and remain the reference set for the future remote-launch phase, if the VLOS pilot proves the concept and the county pursues it. Chinese-made aircraft are excluded. The procurement filter is the current DCMA Blue List and the particular grant's wording, not a vendor's general "NDAA compliant" statement. [[verified]] Manufacturer numbers are not guaranteed field performance: endurance changes with payload, fuel, temperature, altitude, wind, maintenance, and reserves, and Pueblo West's elevation and summer heat make local demonstration testing important. [[verified]]

## What the aircraft must have

Required or strongly preferred: a current grant-eligible Blue List configuration; 640 × 512 thermal or better; stabilized visible-light optical zoom; live optical and thermal video to a remote public-safety operator; encrypted command, telemetry, and video links; an engineered remote link (internet/LTE/mesh), not merely a hand controller beside the drone; multirotor hover and vertical takeoff and landing; human-commanded remote launch; return-to-home; wind tolerance appropriate to Pueblo; manufacturer support; and budget room left for training, communications, enclosure, spares, and contingency. [[proposal]]

Not required for the first pilot: compact folding size; autonomous mission selection; robotic battery charging; camera use while parked; facial recognition; weapons, payload drops, or counter-drone functions. [[proposal]]

## Notes for agents on this page

- No aircraft has been selected, and vendors have not been contacted; the decided sequence puts the website, host conversation, county working group, and grant application before any vendor call.
- Every figure on the cards is the manufacturer's published number as recorded in the research report, checked 2026-08-23; the report's section 8 carries fuller detail per aircraft, including the Perimeter 8+ variant (57 lb, quote-only) and IF1200.
- The Harris H6's exact 25 kg maximum takeoff weight converts to 55.1 lb; Part 107 applies below 55 lb. The card's FAA-path line is the whole issue in one sentence.
- The $40,000 Harris estimate is from a 2025 University of Florida extension comparison, not a quote; always carry that label.
- Product images are deliberately absent until rights and attribution are cleared; the placeholder text is intentional.
- The size silhouettes are approximate scale drawings from published dimensions (Perimeter 8: ~6.5 ft ready to fly; H6: ~2.37 m tip to tip); the C100's is marked unknown because dimensions were not confirmed.
- Source: the research report of 2026-08-23, sections 6, 7, 8; /report.md.
