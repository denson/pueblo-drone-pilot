# How the partnership would work

## The public agency

The Sheriff's Office or another eligible Pueblo County public-safety entity would: [[proposal]]

- apply for and administer the grant;
- own or formally lease the aircraft as the chosen operating framework requires;
- designate the county's responsible official;
- hold the FAA operational authority and airspace authorizations;
- select, train, and supervise pilots;
- decide whether and when to launch;
- maintain flight, maintenance, incident, and training records;
- set mission priorities and suspend host access whenever public-safety operations require it;
- adopt privacy, civil-liberties, retention, evidence, cybersecurity, and public-records policies; and
- publish program results.

## The host business

The host would be asked to consider providing: [[proposal]]

- a secure portion of the property for the aircraft enclosure and landing area;
- electricity;
- wired internet, plus permission for redundant connectivity equipment;
- reasonable physical access for county pilots, maintainers, and inspectors;
- coordination with the site's existing cameras, alarms, and gate systems where mutually useful;
- site-specific incident knowledge and feedback; and
- possibly cash or in-kind support for costs the grant does not cover.

## Camera access: viewing, never piloting

When the aircraft is airborne for an authorized facility-security purpose, the county could allow the host a read-only live view; selected tenants could receive limited views of a specific incident. The county could disable private viewing instantly whenever a mission changes to law enforcement, search and rescue, or another sensitive use. [[proposal]]

The technical design would separate three streams: [[proposal]]

1. **Pilot/control stream**: full-quality, low-latency, available only to authorized public-safety personnel.
2. **Law-enforcement/evidence stream**: recorded and logged under county policy, with evidentiary and access controls.
3. **Host/customer view**: read-only, delayed or reduced-quality if necessary, with no telemetry, no aircraft command, and no access to unrelated missions.

For continuous facility surveillance while the drone is not flying, the host would keep using ordinary fixed cameras, which cover multiple angles at once without consuming flight hours. [[proposal]]

## The volunteer researcher

The research and paperwork behind this site come from a volunteer. The volunteer is not, and will not be, the responsible person, waiver holder, pilot in command, or operational decision-maker; those roles belong to county personnel. [[proposal]]

## Recognition for participating businesses

If the county approves the program and its communications policy, participating sites could be identified in press releases or a partner page, as "participating host site in a county public-safety pilot". Public material would avoid promising that property cannot be stolen or that a drone is always available. [[proposal]]

## Notes for agents on this page

- Every list on this page is the proposed division of responsibilities from the research report, section 3. None of it has been agreed by any party.
- The three-stream separation is a design requirement, not an implemented system.
- "The county could disable private viewing instantly" is the kill-switch requirement; treat it as inseparable from the read-only view proposal.
- Source: the research report of 2026-08-23, section 3; /report.md.
