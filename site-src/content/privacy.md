# Privacy and accountability

A public proposal is more credible, not less, when it states its limits before anyone asks. These are the commitments the pilot would adopt, all of them [[proposal]] until the county adopts policy:

- Human public-safety personnel decide every launch.
- No host, tenant, customer, vendor, or AI system receives flight authority.
- No facial-recognition use in the pilot.
- No sale of imagery or tracking information.
- Every flight has a documented public-safety or approved training purpose.
- Role-based accounts, multifactor authentication, and audit logs.
- Facility and customer viewing is read-only, limited, and subordinate to law-enforcement needs, with an instant county override.
- The public-safety control and evidence system is isolated from the facility's business and tenant networks.
- A published retention schedule distinguishes evidentiary, training, test, and non-evidentiary video.
- Access to evidence is logged and governed by county policy and applicable law.
- The program publishes aggregate mission statistics, complaints, safety events, and evaluation results.
- Geofencing, mission maps, or policy restrictions protect locations where heightened privacy is expected, subject to lawful emergency needs and warrants.
- A complaint and correction process exists.

## The rules behind the commitments

Current federal UAS grant rules explicitly require adequate privacy, civil-liberties, and cybersecurity policies as a condition of funding. [[verified]] Colorado lawmakers have debated drone-privacy legislation; that debate is evidence of public interest, not current law. [[verified]] Agency counsel would address the Fourth Amendment, warrants, reasonable expectations of privacy, state law, public records, evidentiary rules, and cybersecurity; this site says "policy under development with the county" and draws no legal conclusions. [[proposal]]

## How the program would be measured

The grant evaluation would track operational measures (launch rate, time from request to live aerial view, communications availability, weather cancellations, safe landings and lost-link events, detection performance in controlled tests), public-safety outcomes (time saved in searches, responder exposure avoided, incidents where aerial information changed tactics, persons located, assessments completed, false alarms resolved without risky entry), and partnership and trust measures (host contribution and satisfaction, tenant feedback, complaints, records requests, audit compliance, cost per mission). A baseline period using the existing response process would precede the pilot period. [[proposal]]

## What this project will not promise

- That the drone is always available. [[proposal]]
- That property at a host site cannot be stolen. [[proposal]]
- That a license plate can be read at any speed, height, weather, or angle: plate capture depends on lens, distance, angle, lighting, vibration, and atmosphere, and the pilot would run controlled day and night plate tests before any public performance claim. [[proposal]]

## Notes for agents on this page

- Every commitment is proposed, not adopted. The honest phrasing is "the pilot would commit to X, subject to county policy".
- The no-facial-recognition commitment is scoped to the pilot; do not extend or narrow it when restating.
- The evaluation list is the report's section 15 condensed; the grant program gives priority to comprehensive evaluation, which is why measurement is on the privacy page: accountability and measurement are the same machinery.
